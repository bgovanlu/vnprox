T-3504 evidence — what a `fwbr*` bridge actually is on a live node
Host: pvecube, PVE 9.2.4, read-only (`/sys` reads and `grep` of config files)
Captured: 2026-08-20

Why this transcript exists
--------------------------
The Switch view drew `fwbr103i0` and `fwbr104i0` as two large, entirely empty
chassis. T-3504 asks for a disposition — fold, collapse, or hide — and the card
correctly calls that a product judgement. It is only a judgement once you know
what these objects are, and CLAUDE.md forbids answering that from docs or from
internal/pvemock. So it was answered from the node.

What this settles
-----------------
1. **A `fwbr` is one guest NIC's firewall, and its name says which.**
   `fwbr<vmid>i<netid>` has exactly two members, always: `fwln<vmid>i<netid>`
   (the veth leg toward the real bridge) and the guest's own device
   (`veth<vmid>i<netid>` for LXC, `tap<vmid>i<netid>` for QEMU). It is not a
   bridge in the sense the operator means when they make one — it is a
   per-NIC bump in the wire that `pve-firewall` inserts so it has somewhere to
   hang iptables rules. The vmid/netid in the name is a total, lossless mapping
   back to the guest NIC it belongs to.

2. **The operator did not create them, and cannot find them in their config.**
   `/etc/network/interfaces` mentions `fwbr` zero times. They exist only at
   runtime, created at guest start and destroyed at guest stop. `firewall=1` is
   the PVE GUI's *default* when adding a NIC, so an operator who has never
   heard of `fwbr` will still have one per running guest NIC.

3. **They are structurally empty in vnprox's model, and always will be.**
   Their two members are runtime-owned interfaces vnprox deliberately does not
   model as entities (the same class T-3502 taught `internal/drift` to ignore —
   see `pve-9.2.4-firewall-veths.txt`), and the guest NIC's `attached-to` edge
   points at the *logical* bridge the guest is configured for (`vmbr0`), not at
   the `fwbr`. So the captured topology shows `bridge:pvecube:fwbr103i0` with no
   ports, no uplinks, no VLANs and no VNets — not because the collector missed
   something, but because there is nothing there to collect that vnprox models.
   Drawing them as chassis therefore cannot be fixed by populating them.

4. **Existence tracks "running guest with firewall=1", not configuration.**
   Guests 100 and 101 both have `firewall=1` NICs and neither has an `fwbr`,
   because neither is running. Guest 102 (opnsense) has no `firewall` flag on
   any NIC and so has none either — its `net1`/`net2`/`net3` carry
   `link_down=1`, which is the real reason those three render down, unrelated to
   firewalling.

Consequence for the card
------------------------
Of T-3504's three candidate dispositions, two are ruled out by point 3 rather
than by taste: "collapse behind a disclosure" and "hide by default with a
toggle" both keep drawing a device with nothing in it, and merely make the
empty box harder to reach. Only folding the `fwbr` into the guest NIC it
belongs to renders something true — the name already carries the mapping, and
point 1 makes it exact.
